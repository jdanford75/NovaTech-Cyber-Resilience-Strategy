# NovaTech Solutions — Cyber Resilience Strategy

**Academic cybersecurity strategy case study | November 2026 | Jack W. Danford Jr.**

## Project overview

Developed a proposed cybersecurity resilience program for **NovaTech Solutions**, a fictional manufacturing company experiencing ransomware disruption, compromised vendor access, and employee resistance to security changes. The goal was to translate a complex security scenario into a practical, phased plan that protects manufacturing operations while improving risk management.

**This is an academic case study, not a real-company engagement or implemented security program.**

## Topics covered

| Area | Proposed approach |
| --- | --- |
| Ransomware resilience | Incident coordination, protected backups, isolated recovery testing, and safe IT/OT restoration |
| Vendor and third-party risk | Named accounts, phishing-resistant MFA, task-limited permissions, monitored access, and emergency-access review |
| Network and operational technology (OT) | Separation of office and production systems, with changes validated for safety |
| Insider threat | Evidence-based alert review, privacy safeguards, and coordination among IT, HR, and legal |
| Security awareness | Practical, shift-compatible training, supervisor involvement, and accessible MFA support |
| Governance and reporting | Risk register, assigned owners, measurable KPIs, and phased implementation milestones |

## Frameworks and references

The report applies concepts from **NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0**, **NIST SP 800-207 (Zero Trust Architecture)**, and **NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 (Operational Technology Security)**. The original report includes source references.

## Selected recommendations

1. Verify recovery of critical services rather than relying only on backup-success notifications.
2. Restrict vendor access to approved users, systems, tasks, and time windows.
3. Protect production safety when isolating or restoring affected operational technology.
4. Treat insider alerts as investigative leads—not proof of misconduct.
5. Use proposed KPIs and a 30-day, 31–90-day, and 4–12-month rollout plan to track progress.
